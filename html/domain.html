Step 2

Research Gap

Despite significant investment in individual dementia-related AI tools, a critical gap persists between isolated research prototypes and a deployable, clinically meaningful continuous monitoring platform. Our literature review identified five structural limitations across existing systems, each pointing to a distinct unmet clinical need.

01
Modality Isolation, No Cross-Modality Integration

Existing dementia AI tools operate within a single modality, either speech, game performance, clinical questionnaires, or medication reminders, but never in combination. Conversational AI systems do not feed linguistic risk signals into behavioural monitoring systems; MMSE tools do not adjust reminder frequency based on screening outcomes. This siloed architecture means each tool produces data that is never aggregated into a cohesive longitudinal cognitive risk profile.

Clinical impact: Clinicians receive fragmented signals from disconnected systems, no single risk score reflects the full picture of a patient's cognitive trajectory.
02
Snapshot-Only Assessments, No Longitudinal Decline Tracking

Current automated cognitive tools, including digital MMSE platforms and speech-based screeners, provide a single-point-in-time score. They are not designed to compare results across weeks or months, identify rate-of-change in cognitive parameters, or generate trend-based alerts. Yet clinical evidence consistently shows that the trajectory of cognitive decline is more diagnostically significant than any individual assessment score.

Clinical impact: Gradual, early-stage cognitive decline, the most treatable window, goes undetected because no existing system tracks longitudinal cognitive parameter drift in daily life.
03
Non-Adaptive Conversational AI, No Cognitive Risk Framework

LLM-based companions and mental health chatbots deployed for elderly users engage emotionally but do not extract structured cognitive biomarkers from interactions. No existing deployed system concurrently tracks lexical diversity, semantic coherence, pause frequency, topic maintenance, or response latency, the very linguistic parameters clinicians use to identify early dementia markers. Furthermore, none maintain a session-to-session Weighted Estimated Index (WEI) to quantify cognitive change over time.

Clinical impact: Valuable daily conversational data that contains rich cognitive signals is discarded; opportunities for passive, non-intrusive monitoring are lost.
04
Absence of Unified Multi-Modal Platform

No prior work integrates conversational AI, gamified cognitive assessment, automated MMSE screening, and adaptive behavioural reminders into a single, continuously monitored platform with a fused weekly risk score. The clinical community has called for exactly this kind of "ambient intelligence" approach to dementia monitoring, yet research prototypes remain academically compartmentalised. The absence of a unified platform means patients must be referred across multiple separate systems, creating coordination burden for caregivers and clinicians alike.

Clinical impact: Caregivers and clinicians cannot access a single, trusted, comprehensive cognitive risk summary without manually reconciling outputs from disparate tools.
05
Clinical Access Barriers, Stigma & Infrastructure

The stigma associated with formal cognitive testing discourages elderly individuals from seeking regular clinical evaluation. In low-resource and rural settings, specialist memory clinics are geographically inaccessible to large patient populations. Existing digital tools require clinician administration or institutional infrastructure, precluding truly independent, home-based, continuous monitoring. A mobile-first, naturalistic, AI-driven approach that embeds assessment into daily routines without labelling interactions as "tests" represents an unmet design requirement.

Clinical impact: The earliest, most actionable stages of dementia risk go undetected in community-dwelling elderly populations who disengage from formal healthcare pathways.
Step 3

Research Problem

The overarching research problem is grounded in the clinical imperative to detect dementia at the earliest possible stage, before significant neurodegeneration has occurred, through means that are accessible, non-stigmatising, and embedded in everyday life.

Core Research Question

How can a multimodal AI-driven platform continuously monitor cognitive decline indicators in elderly individuals through naturalistic daily interactions, providing early dementia risk detection, empathetic conversational support, and proactive caregiver alerts, without requiring intrusive clinical assessments?

Why This Problem Matters

Dementia affects 55 million people globally, with 10 million new cases annually. The window for meaningful clinical intervention is narrow, early-stage Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) is treatable if detected, but current detection relies on infrequent clinical visits that miss the gradual, naturalistic decline patterns occurring in daily life. A system that passively monitors cognitive health through natural interactions could shift dementia care from reactive crisis management to proactive risk management.

Scope & Boundaries

This research targets community-dwelling elderly individuals aged 60+ who are living independently or with caregivers. The platform focuses on risk estimation rather than clinical diagnosis, it is designed as an assistive early-warning system that flags elevated cognitive risk and supports, rather than replaces, clinical assessment. The system operates across Android, iOS, and web platforms via a Kotlin Multiplatform architecture.

Sub-Research Questions
SQ1 Can 12 cognitive parameters extracted from natural conversation reliably differentiate normal ageing from early dementia risk in real-time?
SQ2 Does gamified cognitive stimulation with motor baseline calibration provide a valid, continuous biomarker of cognitive decline over time?
SQ3 Can multimodal audio-text fusion achieve clinician-comparable MMSE classification accuracy without requiring specialist administration?
SQ4 Can behavioural profiling of reminder interaction patterns detect confusion events with sufficient accuracy and speed to trigger timely caregiver intervention?
Step 4

Research Objectives

Five primary objectives guide the Hale research programme, each directly addressing one of the identified research gaps and contributing to the unified platform architecture. Each objective specifies the technical approach, key models deployed, and measurable success criteria.

01
Emotion-Aware Conversational AI for Cognitive Monitoring

Develop a LLaMA 3.2 3B Instruct model fine-tuned via LoRA on dementia-specific conversational data to conduct empathetic daily dialogues while simultaneously extracting 12 structured cognitive parameters per session. Parameters include: lexical diversity (TTR), semantic coherence, pause frequency, repetition rate, sentiment drift, topic maintenance, response latency, word-finding pauses, syntax complexity, emotional tone, question comprehension, and task completion rate. A BERT + XGBoost pipeline classifies cognitive risk from text features; late fusion with Whisper ASR acoustic features achieves a multimodal accuracy of 89.06%. Risk is tracked across sessions via a Weighted Estimated Index (WEI) to detect longitudinal cognitive change.

02
Gamified Cognitive Stimulation with Temporal Decline Detection

Design a memory card-matching game with an initial motor baseline calibration session that establishes a personalised reaction time and accuracy baseline for each user. Two engineered feature groups, SAC (Session Accuracy Change) and IES (Inverse Efficiency Score), capture within-session and cross-session cognitive performance shifts. An LSTM network models sequential gameplay sessions to detect temporal cognitive decline patterns, achieving 72.21% temporal detection accuracy. A subsequent XGBoost + Random Forest ensemble classifies gameplay features into 3-level dementia risk categories, achieving 96.17% classification accuracy, with session results contributing a weighted sub-score to the WEI.

LSTM Temporal ModellingXGBoost + Random Forest Motor Baseline CalibrationSAC + IES Features 96.17% Classification72.21% Decline Detection
03
Voice-Based Automated MMSE Screening

Build a fully automated, clinician-free MMSE administration system that delivers the 30-point cognitive battery via voice prompts and analyses spoken responses using a two-stream pipeline. The acoustic stream extracts 13 MFCCs, chroma, and spectral centroid features via Librosa, classified by XGBoost. The text stream applies TF-IDF vectorisation with PCA dimensionality reduction, classified by Random Forest. A late fusion strategy combines both stream predictions to output a 4-level cognitive classification (Normal / MCI / Moderate Impairment / Severe Impairment) at 90.70% accuracy, enabling community-based dementia screening without specialist infrastructure.

04
Context-Aware Adaptive Reminder & Confusion Detection

Implement an adaptive reminder system integrating BERT-based Named Entity Recognition (dslim/bert-NER) to parse medication and appointment information from natural language inputs. The system maintains a 30-day rolling behavioural log from which 17 behavioural traits are extracted (response latency, repetitive queries, missed acknowledgements, temporal anomalies, etc.). A Gradient Boosting classifier trained on this log detects confusion events in real time at 96.51% accuracy, with detected events triggering immediate WebSocket-based push alerts to the caregiver React web dashboard, delivering contextual patient status with a mean alert latency of under 1.2 seconds.

05
Three-Tier Distributed Platform with Weekly Risk Fusion

Integrate all four AI modules into a cohesive three-tier distributed architecture: Tier 1: Kotlin Multiplatform mobile app (Android + iOS) with Jetpack Compose UI and MVVM architecture; Tier 2: Python FastAPI cloud backend with MongoDB Atlas, JWT authentication, TLS encryption, and RBAC; Tier 3: React + Tailwind caregiver web dashboard with real-time patient monitoring. The four module sub-scores are aggregated weekly into a Weighted Estimated Index (WEI) (Conversational AI 35%, MMSE 30%, Game 20%, Reminders 15%) producing a 0–100 risk scale with 5 risk levels (Minimal / Low / Moderate / High / Critical). The overall system achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.94.

Step 5

Methodology

A five-stage research pipeline, spanning data collection, module-level AI development, multimodal feature fusion, and integrated risk scoring, underpins the Hale platform. Each module targets a distinct cognitive assessment modality and contributes a weighted sub-score to the unified Weekly Estimated Index (WEI).

00
Dataset & Preprocessing
Multi-source clinical and behavioural corpora
Foundation

Three curated datasets power the training pipeline: the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus (dementia / healthy speech transcripts), the Facebook Empathy Dialog Dataset dataset (emotion and sentiment patterns), and the ADNI dataset (longitudinal neuroimaging and cognitive scores). Audio signals are processed via Librosa (MFCCs, chroma, spectral centroid) and FFmpeg (resampling, noise reduction). Text is tokenised with SpaCy and embedded using BERT base uncased. Class imbalance is corrected with SMOTE oversampling.

Datasets

  • Facebook Empathy Dialog Dataset
  • ADNI (Neuroimaging)
Preprocessing Tools

  • FFmpeg: Audio resampling & denoising
  • SpaCy: NLP tokenisation & NER
  • BERT: Sentence embeddings
  • SMOTE: Class rebalancing
Feature Types

  • TF-IDF text vectors (PCA reduced)
